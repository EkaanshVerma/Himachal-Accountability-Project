Who we are

Born in HP.
Built for HP.

The Himachal Accountability Project is a citizen-run civic infrastructure initiative — built by people who live here, vote here, and use the same broken roads everyone else is reporting. No Delhi office. No corporate hierarchy. No political backing.

Why this exists

Himachal's administrative failures are documented everywhere except one place — a single, public, searchable record.

Social media posts disappear. RTI replies sit in inboxes. News coverage moves on. HAP exists to make sure every logged grievance accumulates — ward by ward, month by month — into a dataset that can't be ignored, dismissed, or buried.

How we operate

Four things we don't compromise on.

01
No political money, ever

We decline contributions from political parties, candidates, elected officials, and their associates — BJP, Congress, AAP, or otherwise. This is structural, not aspirational. The data only means something if no one can buy what it says.

02
Both parties, equal scrutiny

HAP maps failures under whoever is in power at any given time. If a road has been broken for three years across two different governments, both entries go on the record. Selective accountability is just opposition research with a civic coat of paint.

03
Data before claims

Every post, reel, and dossier we publish is traceable to a verified entry in the grievance database — pin code, location, duration, citizen quote. We don't publish what we can't source. The map is only as credible as its sourcing discipline.

04
Open books, always

We publish quarterly transparency reports covering every rupee received and spent. Anyone can request a line-item breakdown. The grievance database is publicly accessible. There is no private layer to this project that contradicts the public one.
